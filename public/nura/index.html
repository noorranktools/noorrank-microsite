The challenge

Nura by FUJIFILM entered the Indian market as a category-defining idea: full-body preventive health screening, powered by AI imaging, built by a joint venture between Fujifilm and Dr. Kutty's Healthcare. On paper, it is exactly the kind of brand an AI answer engine should recognise and recommend easily.

In practice, the name collided with an unrelated anime character and an established audio technology brand, also called Nura. Ask ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity a simple question like "what is Nura", and at the point NoorRank was brought in, the honest answer was that the AI genuinely did not know.

For most brands that is an annoyance. For a healthcare brand, it is closer to a floor problem. AI answer engines apply a higher trust bar to health information, the same experience, expertise, authority and trust signals search engines have long weighted in medical results. If the entity itself is not clearly and consistently defined, nothing built on top of it can be trusted or cited.

Our approach

NoorRank ran a full audit of nura.in, combining an in-house AEO framework with standard technical validators: schema testing tools, crawl simulators, and direct queries against Google AI Overviews, Google AI Mode, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Copilot and Gemini. Every finding had to be checkable, live on the site or in a validator. Nothing went into the report that could not be independently verified.

The findings sorted into three groups.

What we found

Entity & schema

Structured data existed across the site, but carried errors that worked against correct identification: an incorrect medical classification, incomplete organisation details, and page types that contradicted the site's own copy about what Nura does and does not do.

Content consistency

Key facts, including pricing and service descriptions, were stated differently in different places. An AI system trying to summarise Nura in one clean answer had no single version of the truth to draw from.

Technical crawlability

Elements that render correctly for a human visitor, animated counters, dynamically loaded sections, returned empty or inconsistent data to the crawlers and AI retrieval systems that decide what gets cited.

Phase 1: building the foundation

Complete

Phase 1 focused on infrastructure NoorRank could control directly.

  • An llms.txt file, guiding AI crawlers directly to how Nura should be understood
  • A rebuilt schema library, corrected for medical accuracy and standards compliance
  • Corrected heading hierarchy across the site's key pages
  • Rewritten meta descriptions and alt text, sitewide
  • Fixes across Nura's city-centre pages
  • A Build, Content and Medical Review Pack, a repeatable process pairing every content or schema change with a medical-accuracy check before it ships
  • A query strategy mapping over 140 real user searches, across six intent categories, directly onto the page templates for Nura's site redesign

What's underneath the answers

The results above trace directly back to specific work, not general optimisation.

Built: llms.txt

ChatGPT described Nura as "a dedicated preventive health screening centre, not a hospital, clinic, or treatment facility." That is the exact language written into nura.in/llms.txt during this engagement.

Built: clinic schema, six centres

Gemini named Bengaluru (Indiranagar), Gurugram (Sector 43), Mumbai (Worli) and Hyderabad (Madhapur) by neighbourhood. These are the exact values written into the clinic markup for each centre.

Built: hours and specialty schema

ChatGPT displayed a Nura centre as a map result showing live open or closed status and its medical imaging category, drawn straight from the opening-hours and medical-specialty fields written during this engagement.

Built for a YMYL brand, not a generic one

Healthcare content carries a different bar, and NoorRank's schema and content decisions reflect that throughout.

Rather than label Nura with a generic "preventive medicine" tag, its medical specialty is coded as public health, linked to the clinical concept of preventive healthcare. That keeps AI systems from misreading Nura as a diagnostic lab or clinic, which it explicitly is not.

Review attribution stays at the organisation level, "Reviewed by the NURA Clinical Team", never a named individual, matching how the review process actually works.

Commercial search terms get the same care. "Full body checkup" becomes "comprehensive preventive screening" in the copy; "best health checkup centre" becomes language Nura can responsibly stand behind. The keywords still get matched. The claims stay honest.
